# Alessandro M.

In my life I have written poems and songs.  
I have been a busker and a dreamer.

For a long time I searched for words, sounds, and ways to reach people.

The street taught me that communication is not only about having something to say, but about finding the right way to make someone stop and listen.

Then code arrived.

Today I write code for work. I love Python, but I still live it as a language too: made of rhythm, pauses, rules, errors, and possibilities.

Maybe that is why, every now and then, I feel the need to take it outside its natural use.

`print("human")` starts here.

It is the point where, inside me, code, art, and communication meet.

These are not programs to execute, but metaphorical code: small fragments of syntax used to speak about deeply human things.

Because, after all, the medium has changed more than once.

The need has not.

And I still have something else to say.
